About and sources

What CrewTrace shows and where the numbers come from.

Dashboard

Pick an astronaut and see their live health score and vitals, the habitat map, NASA cabin data, alerts, space weather and care.

Astronaut profile

Portrait, symptoms, a clickable body and every health value against its healthy range.

Health guide

Care, food and drink for the chosen astronaut, built from their live values and symptoms.

Data
  • NASA data: cabin CO₂, temperature, humidity and radiation come from NASA OSDR EDA, mission RR-9 (ISS), replayed one record per second. Space weather is a saved set of NASA DONKI and NOAA GOES events for 4–18 Sep 2017, the same days as the radiation replay, built into the page.
  • Scenario: crew names, vitals, symptoms, positions, exposure and dose are a labelled simulation, not real people.
  • One crew, linked pages: the selected astronaut, their live vitals and symptoms are the same on the Dashboard, crew telemetry, Profile and Health guide.
  • Health score: 0 to 100%. Starts from body-area risks, then falls when live heart rate rises, SpO₂ drops, blood pressure climbs or symptoms appear.
  • Alerts flag a shared place and time for checking. They are not a diagnosis.

Sources: NASA OSDR Environmental Data Application (RR-9 cabin and radiation data); space weather: STCE newsletter, 15 Sep 2017 (flares), STCE review of 4–15 Sep 2017 (storms, ground level event), arXiv:1902.03969 (particle events; CME speeds from the NASA DONKI catalog).

Model
  • Bone loss about 1.5% per month at the hips, 1.3% femur, 1.1% shins, 1.0% spine without exercise. Exercise cuts it by up to 70%.
  • Muscle loss rises to about 14% for postural muscle, reduced by exercise.
  • Eye pressure (SANS), plasma volume and standing risk follow saturating curves over mission days (modelling assumptions).
  • Radiation: 0.5 mSv per day baseline plus 0.15 mSv per day for the elevated space weather of September 2017, against a 600 mSv career budget.
  • Healthy ranges: WHO BMI and T-score, AHA blood pressure, age-based body fat, 7 to 9 h sleep, 120 min exercise per day.

Sources: NASA Human Research Program (bone and muscle loss), NASA-STD-3001 radiation technical brief (600 mSv limit), WHO (BMI ranges), American Heart Association (blood pressure), Gallagher et al. 2000 (body fat ranges).

Health guide
  • Medicine names are common options only. No doses: follow the label and ask the flight surgeon, a doctor or a pharmacist.
  • Daily targets follow NASA nutrition requirements: water 2.5 L (men) or 2.1 L (women), vitamin D 25 µg, calcium 1,000 to 1,200 mg, vitamin K 120/90 µg, vitamin C 125/110 mg.
  • Urgent warning signs are always shown.

Sources: NASA exploration nutrition requirements (JSC-67378), NASA Human Adaptation to Spaceflight: The Role of Food and Nutrition, Diaz et al. 2024, ISS medication use, npj Microgravity.
